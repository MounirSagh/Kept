# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Customer Success and Account Managers at B2B companies, plus the heads of CS who own the org in Kept. They open Kept before a customer call, ahead of a renewal, or at the start of the week to see which promises their team made to customers, which are due, and which are at risk. Owners also run the workspace: teams, people, invitations and connected tools.

## Product Purpose

Kept reads a team's email, chats, tickets and CRM, extracts every commitment made to a customer, and flags the ones at risk before the customer notices. Success means no promise slips through the cracks: a CSM can walk into any call knowing exactly what was promised, by whom, and whether it was kept, with the source to prove it.

## Brand Personality

Calm, trustworthy, precise. Kept is the quiet, reliable colleague who never forgets anything. It reports risk with clarity rather than alarm, cites its sources, and stays out of the way. Copy is plain and specific; the interface feels like a well-kept record, not a control room. References: Attio (calm, record-centric, tables that breathe) and Notion (soft, document-like, warm neutrals, minimal color).

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS templates: purple gradients, hero-metric tiles, identical card grids, decorative glass.
- Alarmist dashboards: walls of red, flashing badges, risk presented as panic.
- Playful or consumer styling: illustrations, emoji, bouncy motion. This is about customers' money and trust.

## Design Principles

1. **Evidence over assertion.** Every commitment, flag and recommendation links back to the source it came from. Show the receipt.
2. **Calm urgency.** Surface what's at risk first, with severity carried by position, wording and restrained color, never by alarm.
3. **The record is the interface.** Accounts, commitments and people are first-class records; pages read like well-organized documents, not widget dashboards.
4. **Quiet until needed.** Chrome recedes; content and the next action lead. Empty states explain what will appear and how to get it there.
5. **Respect the hierarchy.** People see exactly what their role allows, and the UI never offers an action the system would refuse.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA. Text contrast at least 4.5:1 (3:1 for large text and UI boundaries), full keyboard navigation with visible focus, `prefers-reduced-motion` respected, and color never the only carrier of status (pair with text or shape).
